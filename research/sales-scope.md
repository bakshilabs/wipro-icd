## Part 2. Topic by topic

### T1. The IT services sales motion and roles

**What the interviewer is testing.** Whether Arpan understands where a practice specialist sits in a matrix that the client partner leads, and whether he can sell without owning the account. Also whether he sells with insight rather than relationship alone.

**Core concepts (general practice)**
- **Client partner / account executive / Global Account Executive (GAE):** owns the account plan and the account's revenue, bookings and margin (wipro-sales.md #3, #85–86).
- **Practice specialist (overlay):** owns a service line's offer in many accounts. Brings solution, proof points and pricing logic. Usually shares order-book credit with the account team.
- **Pursuit lead:** runs a specific large bid from qualification to signature (wipro-sales.md #4, #47, #88).
- **Presales / solution architect:** designs the solution and builds the cost model (wipro-sales.md #45–46).
- **Delivery and engagement manager:** signs off that the solution can be delivered at the stated cost. Owns transition.
- **Consulting / Capco:** originates C-suite demand upstream (wipro-sales.md #5–6, #77).
- **Buying group:** everyone who influences the client's decision, including procurement, architecture, risk and the business owner.

### T2. Deal origination: proactive, reactive, renewals, re-bids, sole source, frameworks, consolidation

**What the interviewer is testing.** Whether Arpan can create demand rather than wait for RFPs, and whether he knows the economics of each source of deals. This follows up round 1's proactive-versus-reactive question.

**Core concepts (general practice)**
- **Proactive deal:** the supplier originates the idea (unsolicited proposal, workshop output, benchmark-led offer, AI pilot). It is often single-sourced or competed on the supplier's terms. Higher win rate and margin, but it needs executive sponsorship.
- **Reactive deal:** the client or its sourcing adviser issues an RFI or RFP. There are more bidders, it is more price-driven, and it often starts with a defined scope.
- **Renewal:** the same scope is extended, often with a price step-down. **Early renewal:** the supplier offers to renew before expiry in exchange for scope expansion or AI productivity.
- **Re-bid / re-compete:** the incumbent defends against a competitive process at expiry.
- **Sole source / direct award:** no competition. It is common for extensions in private sector clients; in the public sector it is restricted by Procurement Act ss.41 and 43.
- **Framework call-off:** a mini-competition or direct award under a pre-qualified framework.
- **Vendor consolidation:** the client cuts its supplier panel and moves scope to fewer suppliers. Large and long, but priced aggressively (wipro-sales.md #59–61).

### T3. Pipeline, forecasting and order book

**What the interviewer is testing.** Whether Arpan can manage a number: build enough pipeline, call a forecast honestly and convert order book into revenue. This was asked in round 1 and is the core of J1.

**Core concepts (general practice unless sourced)**
- **Pipeline coverage:** open qualified pipeline for a period divided by the target. The coverage you need is roughly 1 ÷ win rate (claim 18). At a 33% win rate you need about 3x.
- **Weighted pipeline:** each deal's value multiplied by its stage probability. It is useful for capacity planning but poor for calling a quarter.
- **Forecast categories:** Commit (will close this period; the seller stakes credibility on it), Best case (could close with help), Pipeline (open but not expected this period), Closed won and Closed lost (claim 19).
- **Slippage:** forecast deals that move out of the period. It is the main forecasting failure in services (wipro-sales.md #63, #65).
- **Order book (OB) / bookings:** total contract value (TCV) signed in the period. Wipro counts new orders, renewals and changes (wipro-sales.md #38). It is a management measure, not an accounting one.
- **TCV and ACV:** TCV is the total value over the contract term; ACV is TCV divided by years. Analysts such as ISG track ACV (claim 23).
- **Revenue recognition:** under IFRS 15, revenue is recognised as performance obligations are met. For fixed-price work Wipro uses percentage of completion (deals.md #7).
- **Remaining performance obligations (RPO):** the IFRS 15 disclosure of contracted revenue not yet recognised. It is usually smaller than "order book" because of practical expedients and termination for convenience (claims 21–22).
- **Order book to revenue conversion:** how fast TCV turns into revenue. It depends on ramp (Wipro: 6–8 quarters for large deals, wipro-sales.md #43), tenor and whether the deal is net new or a renewal.

### T7. Commercial models and contract types

**What the interviewer is testing.** Round 1 asked about outcome-based and performance-based contracts. Expect a deeper follow-up: define each model, say who carries the risk, say when to use it and say how it affects margin and revenue recognition. deals.md section 1.1 has the basic table; this section adds definitions and sources.

**Core concepts and the distinctions that matter**
- **Input-based:** the client pays for effort (T&M, rate card, capacity or FTE). The client carries delivery risk.
- **Capped T&M:** T&M with a ceiling. If the cap is reached, the supplier finishes at its own cost (claim 41).
- **Fixed price (firm price if not indexed):** a fixed fee for a defined scope. The supplier carries estimation risk (claim 38).
- **Managed service / capacity / pod:** a fee per service tower, pod or capacity unit with SLAs. Productivity glide paths are common.
- **Output-based (unit) pricing:** the fee is per unit delivered (per test case executed, per ticket, per application migrated, per invoice processed) (claim 42).
- **Performance-based:** the fee is linked to how well the **service** performs against SLAs or KPIs that the supplier controls. Mechanisms are service credits (deductions), earn-back (credits returned if performance recovers) and bonuses. Measures are operational: availability, response time, defect leakage.
- **Outcome-based:** the fee is linked to the **client's business result**, which the supplier only partly controls: cost per claim, conversion uplift, cost-to-serve, revenue (claims 42–43). Payment by results is the government term (claim 44).
- **Gain-share:** a base fee plus a share of measured savings or revenue against a baseline. **Pain/gain or target cost:** both parties share overruns and underruns around a target, often up to a cap (claim 40).
- **Subscription / consumption / platform-plus-service:** recurring charge for a platform or agent capability, sometimes per transaction (wipro-sales.md #79).
- **Hybrid:** a base fee plus outcome kicker, the most-preferred model in the HFS survey (claim 46).

The one-line distinction for the interview: **performance-based pays for how well we run the service; outcome-based pays for what the service achieves for the client's business.** Outcome-based moves more risk to the supplier, needs agreed baselines and measurement, and usually needs a higher margin to compensate (claim 39).

### T8. Contract structure and risk

**What the interviewer is testing.** Whether Arpan can spot the terms that destroy margin and know where Legal needs to be involved. With a banking background, the interviewer will expect awareness of regulatory outsourcing requirements.

**Core concepts (general practice unless sourced)**
- **MSA and SOW:** the master services agreement holds the legal terms; statements of work hold scope, price and deliverables per project.
- **Change control:** the procedure for pricing and agreeing scope changes. It is the main tool for protecting margin after signature.
- **Limitation of liability (LoL):** a cap on damages, often a multiple of annual charges, with super caps (e.g. data protection) and exclusions (fraud, death, often IP indemnity).
- **Indemnities:** promises to cover specific losses (IP infringement, data breach, TUPE liabilities).
- **IP:** who owns bespoke deliverables and who keeps pre-existing tools such as WEGA accelerators. AI outputs raise new questions.
- **Termination:** for cause, for convenience (with termination fees covering unrecovered investment) and on change of control.
- **Step-in:** the client's right to take over a failing service, itself or through a third party.
- **TUPE:** UK rules under which staff move automatically to the new provider in outsourcing, re-tendering or insourcing.
- **Benchmarking:** an independent comparison of price or service with the market, which can trigger price reductions.
- **Indexation / COLA:** annual price changes linked to an index or wage inflation.
- **Exit management:** exit plan, transition assistance and data return.

### T9. Pricing and margin

**What the interviewer is testing.** Whether Arpan can build or challenge a price, protect margin and explain AI deflation. J7 and J8 are explicitly about margin.

**Core concepts (general practice unless sourced)**
- **Cost build-up:** effort by role × location × cost rate + tools, licences, travel, transition, governance + contingency + risk premium + margin.
- **Pyramid:** the mix of senior to junior staff. A flatter pyramid costs more. deals.md #13 gives a rule of thumb (unverified).
- **Location mix:** onshore, nearshore and offshore shares. Wipro was 62.2% offshore in Q2 FY27 (deals.md #9).
- **Utilisation:** billed time as a share of available time. Wipro was 83.4% (deals.md #10).
- **Contingency:** an allowance for estimation uncertainty. **Risk premium:** a price added for commercial risk such as credits, liquidated damages or caps (deals.md #29, unverified).
- **Discount governance and margin approvals:** prices below standard margin need approval at rising levels. Wipro's thresholds are not public (wipro-sales.md #48).
- **AI productivity pass-through:** how much of an AI efficiency saving goes to the client as a lower price.
- **Wage inflation and indexation (COLA):** offshore wage rises are absorbed by the supplier unless the contract indexes prices.

### T11. Negotiation

**What the interviewer is testing.** Whether Arpan can hold margin with a professional procurement team and sourcing adviser, and whether he plans concessions.

**Core concepts**
- **BATNA:** best alternative to a negotiated agreement. **Reservation price:** your walk-away point. **ZOPA:** the zone of possible agreement between both sides' reservation prices (claim 73).

- **Multi-year step-downs (general practice):** annual price reductions, ideally tied to productivity delivered and volume commitments.

**What the interviewer is testing.** Whether Arpan understands how the biggest order-book numbers are made and their risks. J11 says "large-scale".

**Core concepts (general practice)**

### T15. AI's effect on services sales

**What the interviewer is testing.** Whether Arpan can sell AI-led deals without destroying revenue, and whether he understands the economics: productivity commitments, outcome pricing, token costs and platform-plus-service. This is where his AI training background is an asset.

**Core concepts (general practice unless sourced)**

### T16. Transition, delivery handover and deliverability

**What the interviewer is testing.** He rose from delivery. He will test whether Arpan sells things that can be delivered at the stated margin, and how he hands over to delivery.

**Core concepts (general practice)**
- **Sales-to-delivery handover:** a formal pack of solution, assumptions, commitments, risks, client expectations and contacts, with the delivery lead involved before signature.
