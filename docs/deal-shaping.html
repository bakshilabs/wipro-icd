AI productivity

Evidence on AI productivity

Controlled studies disagree widely. The results run from experienced developers taking 19% longer to a 55.8% speed-up on a well-defined coding task. I would apply AI productivity by task type and state it as an explicit assumption in the price.

Move 1: expand

Why expansion deals pay

Move 1 of my plan grows IC&D scope inside accounts where Wipro already runs work. These deals tend to cost less to win and sign sooner, and they can offset the renewal pressure shown above. Each point is marked as sourced or as my judgement.

Phased first scopes

A fixed-price diagnostic or pilot on one domain inside a live estate can be signed quickly and books order book early. The next phase is priced on evidence from the first.

My judgement

Add-ons to run contracts

Renewal step-downs have moved from 3–4% a year to closer to 6–7% (Pzena). TCS offers early renewal "with greater AI infusion, greater productivity delivered, and at the same time, with expansion in scope". Adding QE, modernisation or AI scope at renewal offsets the step-down.

Sourced

Productivity shared through scope

Analysts cite "pricing pressure in renewals due to client expectations of higher productivity pass-through" (Kotak, via ANI). Passing part of the AI gain to the client in exchange for added scope keeps the account's value growing. One research blog reports TCS passes 10–15% of savings at signing and recovers it with added scope (to confirm).

SourcedOne figure to confirm

Lower cost of sale

The client partner already holds the relationship, the delivery team knows the estate and the live service supplies the data for the business case. HBR summarises studies finding that a new customer costs 5 to 25 times more to acquire than an existing one costs to keep.

Margin protection

How I would protect margin

The levers I would work on with Delivery and Finance from qualification to contract, in the order they usually come up. The calculator above shows the size of each one on a given deal.

  1. ScopeDefine outcomes and exclusions early, phase discovery before build, and keep a written assumptions and dependencies schedule. Change control covers anything outside it. Fixed price suits scopes where the specification is complete, which is the test Infosys states in its 20-F.
  2. PyramidAgree the grade mix with Delivery against the work itself, so senior time goes to architecture, client leadership and review, and repeatable work goes to mid and junior grades.
  3. Onshore and offshore mixKeep onshore roles where the client relationship, regulation or workshops need them, and place the rest offshore. Wipro delivered 62.2% of services revenue offshore in Q2 FY27.
  4. AI-assisted delivery with shared productivityEstimate AI productivity by task type, for example boilerplate, tests, documentation and migration, and write the factor into the price. Under fixed, capacity or outcome pricing, agree what share of the gain passes to the client and when. WEGA and the Claude and Gemini programmes give the delivery basis for these commitments.
  5. Contingency and risk premiumPrice an explicit contingency on fixed-price and outcome work, sized to requirement maturity, and a separate premium for liquidated damages, service credits or uncapped liability. HM Treasury's Green Book guidance on optimism bias makes the same point for public projects.
  6. Commercial guardrailsAgree with Finance a margin floor, the discount authority at each level, rate indexation, payment milestones and liability caps before the first price goes to the client. Under the Procurement Act 2023, contracts above £5m need at least three KPIs, and the three most material are published. I would tie outcome pricing to those measurable KPIs.

At Foster + Partners I led pitches and bids winning more than £10m a year in fees, and pitched, negotiated and won a multi-year contract with a global pharmaceutical company. At Atlas Learn I run sales, marketing and commercial operations.
