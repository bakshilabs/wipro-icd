The role

What Wipro is hiring for

The job description combines five responsibilities. The interview is a senior consultative sales conversation: find the client's business problem, shape a technology-led answer, mobilise Wipro's teams and convert the pursuit into profitable revenue.

Five responsibilities

  1. 1
    Generate revenue and order bookDevelop opportunities, expand existing accounts, hunt new business, keep a qualified pipeline and convert pursuits into signed contracts.
  2. 2
    Shape strategic transformation dealsUnderstand the client's business challenge and combine cloud, application modernisation, digital experience, AI and software engineering into one proposition.
  3. 3
    Build executive relationshipsEstablish credibility with CIOs, CTOs, Chief Digital Officers, customer experience leaders and business executives.
  4. 4
    Protect deal economicsBuild commercially viable proposals, manage scope and pricing, work with Finance and Delivery, and hold margin while staying competitive.
  5. 5
    Orchestrate Wipro's capabilitiesBring account teams, solution architects, global practices, delivery organisations and technology partners together to win and run engagements.

My positioning connects all five. Sales ownership, executive engagement and commercial judgement carry the conversation. Enough technical depth to shape a credible solution and challenge an assumption sits behind them.

Themes to expect

  1. 1
    Business growthHow I build pipeline and expand strategic client relationships.
  2. 2
    Large-deal leadershipHow I influence qualification, solution design, proposals, pricing and closure.
  3. 3
    Transformation credibilityHow I turn AI, customer experience and modernisation into business outcomes.
  4. 4
    Commercial disciplineHow I avoid underpricing, uncontrolled scope and deals that look attractive and deliver at a loss.
  5. 5
    Leadership without direct authorityHow I align sales, technical teams, partners and delivery leaders.

The job description asks for an understanding of one or more technologies, so I go deep where I have delivered and sell the rest with Wipro's practices and partners.

Wipro's stated position

Wipro describes itself as consulting-led, AI-powered and industry-focused. Its digital proposition covers connected customer journeys, AI-enabled experiences, digital commerce, integration and application simplification. Its cloud portfolio covers advisory, migration and modernisation, cloud-native engineering and AI.

The Q2 FY27 release pairs low growth with a stable margin and names larger transformation deals as the priority. Profitable growth, pipeline conversion and differentiated AI-led transformation are the themes to carry.

Draft line to test

What interests me about this role is connecting Wipro's established engineering and modernisation capabilities with consulting-led, AI-powered transformation. I would focus on opportunities where we can show business value, differentiate the proposition and build profitable, lasting client relationships.

Read before the interview: Wipro UK and Ireland, Wipro Digital, Wipro Cloud.

"How would you grow Wipro's Industry Cloud & Digital business in the UK and Ireland?"

The most important question to prepare. The answer is a repeatable growth model, with the site's own strategy as the worked example.

Growth model

  1. 1
    Segment and prioritise accountsStrategic accounts with transformation spend, legacy complexity, AI ambition, weak digital journeys or cost pressure. Start in the sectors where my domain credibility is strongest.
  2. 2
    Build an executive point of viewHypotheses on each client's priorities, taken to the right stakeholders with insight and discovery workshops.
  3. 3
    Create qualified opportunitiesDiscovery becomes a quantified business case, an agreed problem statement, access to decision-makers, a credible budget and a route to a buying decision.
  4. 4
    Build and win integrated propositionsThe relevant Wipro capabilities, technology partners where they add, a differentiated solution and a viable commercial model.
  5. 5
    Expand through measured outcomesDeliver the first commitment, show the value and use the results to open adjacent work across the account.

The model with Wipro's figures: M3 Strategy, M4 Banking, M5 Plan.

Draft answer to test

I would start with Wipro's existing UKI account base, the current pipeline, the strategic relationships and the portfolio's growth priorities. I would segment accounts by addressable opportunity and buying readiness, and find where AI, application modernisation, cloud and digital experience solve a material business problem.

I would work with Client Partners to map stakeholders, understand incumbents and find where to displace, complement or expand existing services. Executive workshops would move each account from a first challenge to a quantified business case and a qualified pursuit.

I would then run each opportunity through qualification, solution design, commercials and closure, watching win probability and margin. Progress is measured in qualified pipeline, conversion, bookings, revenue, profitability and expansion inside existing accounts.

"How would you identify and qualify an opportunity?"

The answer shows the difference between an interesting conversation and a sales opportunity. Six dimensions, each with the question it settles.

DimensionWhat I establish
Business problemWhich measurable business outcome is the client trying to improve?
Strategic relevanceWhy does this matter now, and what happens if they do nothing?
StakeholdersWho sponsors the initiative, owns the budget and makes the decision?
Commercial viabilityIs funding available, and is there a realistic buying process and timeline?
Solution fitDoes Wipro have differentiated capability, delivery credibility and the right partners?
Competitive positionWho else is competing, what is the incumbent's advantage and how does Wipro differentiate?

Before committing pursuit resources I also test delivery feasibility, margin expectations, procurement requirements, dependencies and risks. Qualification rests on a credible sponsor, a business case and a realistic path to closure. A large contract value on its own counts for none of these. The scored version is on A11 Pursuits.

"How would you sell an AI-led transformation to a client?"

The answer moves from business strategy to a safe, measured implementation. The worked example is a UK bank or insurer improving customer servicing. Which model is most impressive never comes into it.

Five steps

  1. 1
    DiscoverCustomer-contact drivers, digital journey drop-offs, resolution times and servicing costs.
  2. 2
    PrioritiseOne high-volume, well-understood use case with measurable value and manageable risk.
  3. 3
    DesignJourney redesign, integration with core systems, retrieval from trusted enterprise information and AI-assisted responses or workflows.
  4. 4
    PilotA baseline, a controlled scope, human escalation, security, data protection and model evaluation criteria agreed up front.
  5. 5
    ScaleExpansion only after accuracy, service quality, adoption, economics and operational controls are shown.

Measures

Cost per contactFirst-contact resolutionCustomer satisfactionDigital adoptionAverage handling timeContainment rate

"How do you protect margin when a client demands a lower price?"

The job description names margin-positive deals, so the answer has a structure.

Draft answer to test

I would first establish whether the objection is about price, scope, perceived value or budget. I would then look at the solution and delivery model for where we can simplify scope, standardise components, automate work, change the delivery mix or phase the engagement without losing the essential outcomes.

I would present options with their cost and value in the open, and discount last. Any concession comes with a matching benefit: a longer commitment, reduced scope, volume certainty or a different allocation of risk.

Before committing I would align with Delivery and Finance on the delivery assumptions, the risks, the margin floor and the contractual protections. If the deal cannot be delivered profitably, I challenge the structure instead of booking it at any cost.

"How would you approach a legacy modernisation opportunity?"

The core assessment and delivery approach, which I can explain whatever my strongest specialism is.

  1. 1
    Discover the estateApplications, business criticality, costs, dependencies, technology risks and user pain points.
  2. 2
    Rationalise the portfolioWhich applications to retain, retire, consolidate or modernise. Wipro offers Application Portfolio Rationalisation inside its cloud economics and modernisation work.
  3. 3
    Set a path for each applicationRehost, replatform, refactor, rearchitect, replace or retire, on business value and technical grounds.
  4. 4
    Build the business caseCosts, benefits, risk, migration effort and time to value, compared.
  5. 5
    Phase the roadmapArchitecture, data, integration, security, quality engineering, DevOps and the operating-model changes.
  6. 6
    Measure the outcomeRun cost, release speed, reliability, technical debt and business agility.

Six paths

RehostReplatformRefactorRearchitectReplaceRetire

Each application gets its own path from its business value, dependencies, constraints and expected return. A rewrite of the whole estate is never the proposal.

"How do you work with Account Managers, Delivery and solution teams?"

The answer shows the difference between owning an opportunity and doing everything myself.

What a good answer contains

  • Shared qualification criteria.
  • Clear roles and accountability for each pursuit.
  • Delivery involved early.
  • A solution developed jointly.
  • Frequent pursuit reviews.
  • Open communication about risks.

Influence without formal authority

I resolve disagreements constructively, keep the client proposition coherent and escalate trade-offs early enough that there are no commercial surprises.
