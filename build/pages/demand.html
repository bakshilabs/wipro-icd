UK and Ireland demand

Demand

This page sets out why UK and Irish organisations are buying digital, CX, AI and modernisation work now, sector by sector. It draws on regulators, official statistics, government reviews and trade press, and ends with what each sector means for a pursuit.

Sources retrieved on 27 October 2026. Sector scores are my judgement from the evidence. Items with a single secondary source are marked "to confirm".

Banking regulation in Europe and the UK

Banks in the EU, Ireland and the UK face resilience, payments and AI rules on fixed dates from 2025 to 2028. Each item below is also on the timeline above. The dates and requirements come from the regulators; the work each one points to is my reading.

Banking rules by jurisdiction

Payments carry the most dates, with euro instant payments, Swift and CHAPS ISO 20022 rules and the PSR all falling between 2025 and 2028.

Critical ICT third-party providers designated on 18 November 2025

The ESAs designated 19 providers for direct EU oversight, and six of the eleven named in the ESAs' release are IT services firms. The grouping is my judgement.

EU banks keep DORA registers, exit plans and contract terms for every ICT provider they use, designated or not, and in 2026 the ESAs are reviewing contracts and service levels between designated providers and EU financial entities. I would expect bank buyers to ask for DORA-ready contract terms and resilience test evidence in any pursuit (my reading). Source: ESMA and ESAs designation release and list, November 2025 (primary); EBA 2026 work programme (primary).

Pursuits

What this means for a pursuit

For each sector: the forcing events that give a reason to call an existing account (Move 1: expand) or a new logo (Move 2: new logos), which accounts I would expand first and which new logos I would target, who I would expect to buy, the dated pressures, the offerings I would lead with, the public Wipro proof I could cite and an opening message. The account types are illustrative archetypes. Buyers and offering choices are my judgement. The messages are drafts to test with account teams and clients.

Shaping opportunities

Turning demand into large deals

The dates above give each account a timetable. My approach is to start from the client's dated obligations, size the work with the practices and bring a combined proposition to the client partner before an RFP exists.

Start from a date

For each target account I would list the forcing dates that apply to it and the internal programme that has to meet each one. That list becomes the first conversation with the client partner.

Shape before the RFP

At Foster + Partners I led pitches and bids that won more than £10m a year in fees and presented to Fortune 500 chief executives. Here I would use executive workshops to shape the brief with the client before a formal tender.

Join the offerings up

Most sectors score 4 or 5 in more than one column. I would combine offerings, for example CX with modernisation and QE, so one programme answers several dated pressures at once. The Portfolio page shows how.
