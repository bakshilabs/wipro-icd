    Entry offer

    Quality engineering and payments resilience

    I would open with payments testing because it meets the nearest dates, it is a contained first contract, and it puts Wipro inside the change process that every later phase runs through. The proof below is limited to public sources.

    What the first contract covers

    • ISO 20022 regression packs for CHAPS and cross-border flows, covering structured addresses now and purpose codes for November 2027.
    • End-to-end payment testing across CHAPS, Faster Payments and Bacs, and euro instant payments with Verification of Payee for the Irish subsidiary.
    • Scenario tests against impact tolerances for the "make a payment" service, with chaos tests on its third-party dependencies.
    • Release evidence packs that feed PS26/2 incident reporting from March 2027 and the Irish DORA register.
    • GenAI test design with IntelliAssure Virtual Automation Engineer, with a tester reviewing every generated script.

    Commercial shape (my judgement)

    A fixed-price assessment and proof on one payment flow, then a managed testing service priced per release or per test cycle.

    Draft message to test

    "Your next two CHAPS changes and the March 2027 reporting rules all land on the same payment systems. We would put automated regression and release evidence around one payment flow in six weeks, show you the defect and cycle-time data, and then price the rest of the estate on that evidence."

    Who hears it first

    The Head of Payments and the Head of Quality Engineering, introduced by the Client Partner. The COO hears it at the executive workshop.

      Executive workshop

      The first workshop at this bank

      A half-day session on payments resilience and quality, sponsored by the COO. It combines a service blueprint of one payment flow with a value against feasibility scoring, two formats from the Workshops page. Select a block for its purpose, who leads it and what it produces.

      Half-day agenda, 210 minutes

      The service blueprint takes the largest block, because it produces the scope. Select a block.

      Who attends

      • COO as sponsor
      • Head of Payments
      • Head of Quality Engineering
      • CIO or a delegate from engineering
      • Head of operational resilience or third-party risk
      • Irish subsidiary technology lead, remotely if needed
      • Wipro: Client Partner, me, a QE practice lead, a payments architect, and Capco if the bank wants a regulatory view

      Pre-reads

      • A two-page outside-in brief with the dated events above
      • The bank's own request: 12 months of payment incidents, rejected payments since November 2026, release frequency and regression cycle time
      • One page on how GenAI test design works and how scripts are reviewed

      What it produces

      • A blueprint of one payment flow with test and evidence points marked
      • Baseline measures the bank agrees with
      • The top two scopes for a six-week proof
      • A named sponsor, a data access route and a decision date, which update the MEDDPICC readings

      Draft message to test, invitation from the Client Partner. "Between now and November 2027 your payment systems take two CHAPS changes, new incident and third-party reporting, and for your Irish bank DORA testing and supervision of ICT change. We would like to spend a morning with you and your payments, engineering and resilience leads, map one payment flow end to end, and agree where better testing evidence would cut risk and release time. You would leave with a scoped six-week proof and a view of what it would cost."

      How I run sessions like this. At SOM in New York I presented to executives at major Wall Street banks. At Atlas Learn I have run board-level AI training for a FinTech and trained more than 250 senior professionals on Claude Code, Cowork and other AI tools. I would run the AI segment hands-on, with the bank's own payment requirement as the input.
