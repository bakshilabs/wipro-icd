# Writing and content rules

The site is shown on Arpan's screen in a panel interview for the ICD Practitioner role at Wipro (Industry Cloud & Digital, UK and Ireland) and then shared with the panel, who may pass it to colleagues. Every page must read well to someone at Wipro who opens it later without Arpan there.

## Who is reading
- A Wipro sales, practice or delivery leader who knows Wipro's offerings, clients and numbers better than any public source does.
- Treat what we found as an outside reading from public sources. Say what it is based on and what would confirm it.
- Do not tell them how their company works. Say what the public sources show, then what I would check, ask or do.
- Write in the first person (I) for Arpan. Call the company Wipro. Do not write "the panel", "he", "the interviewer" or stage directions.
- Do not use Wipro's logo, brand colours or anything that could look like an official Wipro document.

## How to write
- Plain words and short sentences, usually under 20 words. One idea per sentence.
- Headings name the topic: "Who buys", "Pricing the work", "Qualifying a pursuit". They are not claims, slogans or jokes.
- Ledes are one or two plain sentences that say what the page contains and what it is based on.
- Prefer names, sources and concrete nouns to adjectives. Numbers and dates may be used where a source supports them.
- Mark judgement as judgement: "My estimate", "Placeholder figures", "To check with your data".
- British spelling. Full sentences in body copy.

## Do not write
- Appositive title fragments ("X, One Y"). Titles are plain nouns: "Deal Shaping", "Buyers".
- Punch lines, closing twists or a final sentence that sums up what was just said.
- "Not X, but Y", "X, not Y", "rather than" contrasts, or "it's not about X, it's about Y".
- Em dashes. Use a full stop or a comma.
- Rhetorical questions, "here's the thing", "the real question", "the short answer".
- Hype or filler: powerful, seamless, unlock, game-changing, crucial, key (as an adjective), robust, leverage, journey (except "customer journey" as a CX term), landscape, ecosystem, deep dive, cutting-edge, best-in-class, world-class, synergy.
- Lists of three for rhythm. Use as many items as there are.
- Absolute claims about Wipro's internals, people, revenue or strategy beyond what a cited source says.
- Information about named individuals other than chief executives, or anything taken from personal LinkedIn profiles.

## Presenting my experience
- Never show gaps, weaknesses or missing experience, on any page. No "gap", "where I am lighter", "to build" or "what I would learn" sections about me.
- Map each responsibility to the experience and work that answers it, stated plainly and accurately. Do not invent experience.
- Where a responsibility rests on technology I have not delivered myself (mainframe, Java/.NET, QE, DevOps), show how I sell and shape it with Wipro's practices, delivery teams and alliance partners.

## Message lines
Example messaging (value propositions, workshop invitations, objection replies) is part of the work and may stay persuasive. Label it as a draft to test: "Draft message to test".

## Showing on screen
- Anything important must be visible on the page without hovering. Tooltips only add detail.
- Each chart has a one-sentence plain reading under its title.
- Placeholder figures in models are labelled as placeholders, with a line inviting the reader to enter their own.
- Every page ends with a Sources panel. Mark sources as primary or press.
